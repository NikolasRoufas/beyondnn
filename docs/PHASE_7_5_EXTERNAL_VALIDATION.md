## 2. Ground truth and what it can and cannot show

Per (node, sample), the source sample is the next sample.

- **Necessary:** a Tracr (HL) interchange of the node changes the HL argmax at the target position.
- **Not necessary:** non-circuit nodes (the SIIT premise).
- **Ambiguous:** an independent TransformerLens resample ablation of the trained (LL) model disagrees with that label. Ambiguous instances are never counted in TP/FP/TN/FN.

**Limitation found at analysis time (not a deviation; no rule was changed):**
- The PRIMARY configuration is the benchmark's own semantics:
  - a resample of the same LL node from the same source sample;
  - no null;
  - `min_drop` = clean margin, which means "the argmax changes".
- On clear instances, the LL resample agrees with the label *by the definition of "clear"*.
- **Consequences for EH1 and EH2 (the PRIMARY confusion on clear instances):**
  - They are therefore largely **by construction**.
  - They verify that BeyondNN's intervention, faithfulness and audit machinery reproduces an independent TransformerLens computation, and that the audit passes that evidence through without spurious exclusions or findings.
  - They do **not** show that the audit discovers which mechanism is correct.
- **What E1 does test independently:**
  1. the non-PRIMARY configurations: mean and zero replacements, count null, thresholds (RQ4, RQ5);
  2. the attribution-selected heads (EH3), evaluated per selection;
  3. the structural refusal of attribution-only evidence (EH6);
  4. what the audit reports on ambiguous instances (RQ3).
- This is recorded as a must-fix for a NeurIPS-level claim in `research/PHASE_7_5_PAPER_READINESS.md`.

**RQ5:** the same "is this node necessary?" question gets different answers under reasonable replacements.
- **Zero ablation:** supports 13% of not-necessary nodes and misses 30% of necessary ones.
- **Mean replacement:** misses 32% of necessary nodes.
- **The audit's role:** it keeps the PRIMARY standing on the declared semantics, and marks each disagreeing configuration as a `stress_test_reverses` / `alternative_reverses` finding rather than letting any single configuration decide.

**RQ4:**
- The count null used for heads (random head sets of the same size, 20 controls, 0.95) rejects 98.6% of known-necessary heads. The likely reason is that in these 4-head models random heads are often themselves circuit heads, so the control is not a null. This matches the development prediction, and **the policy was not changed**.
- The null is ALTERNATIVE, so the result shows as a qualifying reversal and not as a CONTRADICTED standing: under the declared roles, the over-restrictive null does not reject the known-correct interpretation.
- A plan that had declared this null PRIMARY would have rejected 98.6% of correct claims. The audit does not detect that on its own.

### 3.2 Every PRIMARY error, and every non-standard outcome, looked at individually

- **PRIMARY FP / FN:** none on clear instances.
- **INCONCLUSIVE (9, all case 110, heads L0.H1 and L0.H2):**
  - the resample replacement is a no-op: the source sample's head output equals the clean one, so the PRIMARY test is INCONCLUSIVE (`inconclusive_results`);
  - the zero and mean configurations CONTRADICT;
  - this is correct behaviour: an intervention that changes nothing tests nothing.
- **Ambiguous (18):** all are *non-circuit* nodes that the trained model nevertheless uses:
  - case 21: L0 heads and L0 MLP, 17 instances;
  - case 58: L1.H3, 1 instance.

  The LL resample flips the output there, and the PRIMARY standing is SUPPORTED. The audit reports the trained model's behaviour, not the benchmark's intended circuit. Under an HL-only ground truth these 18 would count as FPs (18/8,858 = 0.2%). In fact they are benchmark imperfections: the SIIT model does not implement the Tracr circuit exactly.
- **Largest STRESS_TEST (zero) FP clusters:** whole nodes, on all or nearly all 40 samples:
  - case 14: L1.H3 (40), L1 MLP (39);
  - case 25: L0.H1, L0.H2 (40 each);
  - case 103: L0 MLP (38);
  - case 63: L1.H0, L1.H1 (37 each).

  These are nodes whose zero output is far from any natural activation: out-of-distribution ablation, the textbook failure mode of zero ablation.
- **Largest ALTERNATIVE (mean) FP clusters:** at most 8 of 40 samples per node (case 129 L1.H0; case 25 L0.H0 / L0 MLP). They are sample-specific, not node-wide.

### 3.3 RQ3: does sensitivity track ambiguity? (descriptive, fd3)

| group | node instances with a reversal finding |
|---|---|
| clear (held-out) | 2,813/9,782 = 28.8% [27.9, 29.7] |
| ambiguous (held-out) | 16/18 = 88.9% [67.2, 96.9] |
| development reliable case 7 | 121/400 = 30.3% [26.0, 34.9] |
| development unreliable case 13 | 157/400 = 39.3% [34.6, 44.1] |

- Reversals are about three times as frequent on ambiguous instances, and more frequent on the unreliable case.
- They are also frequent on clear instances (29%). Much of this comes from the STRESS_TEST zero and ×1.5-threshold configurations, which the declared roles mark INFORMATIONAL (`stress_test_reverses`) and keep out of the standing.
- **Answer:**
  - sensitivity is enriched where the benchmark is genuinely ambiguous;
  - the raw reversal count alone would over-report uncertainty on clear instances;
  - the declared roles are what keep the standing stable there.

## 4. E2: concepts with known use (held-out case 39; weak held-out)

**Concepts:**
- **K+:** the Tracr `is_x` variable, a known positive that is both encoded and used.
- **K−:** `tokens:a`, decodable but unused (HL only).
- **K0:** K+ with permuted labels, a known negative.

**Frozen concept policy:** POLICY_V1; covariance null PRIMARY, isotropic null ALTERNATIVE.

| model / concept | known | encoding (cov / iso) | use | VALIDATED? (cov / iso) | audit (strict / lenient caps) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tracr HL / K+ | positive | supports / supports | **contradicts** (effect −0.062 < 0.1) | no / no | UNSUPPORTED (`decodable_not_used`) |
| Tracr HL / K− | decodable-unused | contradicts / contradicts | contradicts | no / no | UNSUPPORTED |
| Tracr HL / K0 | negative | contradicts / contradicts | contradicts | no / no | UNSUPPORTED |
| SIIT LL / K+ | positive | supports / supports | **contradicts** (−0.064) | no / no | UNSUPPORTED |
| SIIT LL / K0 | negative | contradicts / contradicts | contradicts | no / no | UNSUPPORTED |

**Hypotheses:**
- **KE1 held:** no known negative was validated or SUPPORTED.
- **KE2 held:** Tracr K+ was not validated under the PRIMARY covariance null. It was predicted from development, where K+ encoding failed the covariance null and passed the isotropic one.
- **KE3 (descriptive):** SIIT K+ encodes under both nulls, but its use test fails.

**Reading:**
- On held-out case 39, **the known-positive concept is rejected**: its removal changes the target by about 0.06, below the frozen `min_change` = 0.1.
- The policy is therefore **too strict for a known-used variable in this setting**: a false negative for the concept framework (RQ4 for concepts).
- The development case 3 already showed K+ validated only under the isotropic null. We did not relax 0.10 / 0.95 (request §21), so the result stands as a limitation.
- A known positive that the policy cannot validate means **concept validation has no demonstrated true positive on an external known mechanism**.

## 5. Summary against RQ1–RQ5

- **RQ1 (adequate vs inadequate evidence for a known causal mechanism):**
  - Under the benchmark's own semantics, yes, but by construction (§2).
  - Under alternative replacements, TP falls to 68–70% (recorded thresholds). The audit reports this as reversals rather than as a verdict change.
- **RQ2 (wrong mechanism):** yes, for attribution-selected wrong heads (1,571/1,571 CONTRADICTED) and for attribution-only evidence (UNSUPPORTED, structurally).
- **RQ3:** reversal findings are enriched on ambiguous instances (89% vs 29%), but not specific to them.
- **RQ4:**
  - A declared-ALTERNATIVE over-restrictive null does not reject known-correct claims.
  - A PRIMARY one would. The audit cannot tell an over-restrictive null from a true negative without external knowledge.
  - For concepts, the frozen policy rejects the known positive.
- **RQ5:** yes. The same node is supported under one replacement and contradicted under another (zero: 13% FP; mean: 32% FN), and the per-configuration table and findings make this explicit.
